# Trademark policy

The LocalCut AI **source code** is Apache-2.0, and that licence is deliberately
permissive: fork it, ship it, sell it, build a product on it. This document is
about the one thing the code licence does not give away — the **name and the
mark** — and about the single question it exists to answer for you: *can people
tell your build apart from ours?*

Apache-2.0 says so itself. Section 6 grants no rights in the licensor's trade
names or trademarks. This page just makes the boundary usable rather than
leaving you to infer it.

## What the marks are

- The name **LocalCut** and **LocalCut AI**.
- The logo in [`branding/logo.svg`](branding/logo.svg), and the app icons
  generated from it.

## What you can always do, no permission needed

- Use the software for anything, including commercially.
- Fork the code and distribute your fork under Apache-2.0.
- **Say truthfully what your thing is.** "Based on LocalCut AI", "a fork of
  LocalCut AI", "compatible with LocalCut AI" — nominative use is fine, and we
  will not come after accurate description.
- Write about it, review it, teach it, benchmark it, criticise it.
- Keep the mark in place in an **unmodified** build you redistribute.

## What needs a different name

If you distribute a **modified** build, give it your own name and your own
icon. Concretely, don't:

- publish a modified build still called "LocalCut" or "LocalCut AI";
- list an app in any store under a name that a reasonable person would confuse
  with LocalCut;
- use the logo as your product's icon or as your own branding;
- use the marks in a way that suggests we endorse, sponsor, maintain or
  support your build when we do not;
- register the marks, or anything confusingly similar, as a trademark, company
  name, or domain.

The line is confusion, not modification. Change whatever you like in the code;
just don't ship it as though it were ours.

## Why this exists

Under a permissive licence, the name is the only thing that connects a
reputation to a build. If a modified LocalCut can be sold as LocalCut, then a
bad fork's bugs, telemetry, or licensing choices become our support burden and
our reputation, and no user has a way to tell the difference. Reserving the
name is what keeps the code free to take.

That is the whole of the intent. This is not a policy designed to make
downstream distribution awkward — if you are trying to do something reasonable
and this document appears to forbid it, ask.
